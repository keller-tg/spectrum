## Grouped by axis, with explainers

### 1. Distribution: Equality ↔ Markets

*Measures:* How far the state should redistribute income and wealth through taxes, transfers and wage rules.

- **#2** (Quick, agree → Equality) Governments should actively reduce the gap between high and low incomes.
  - ?: “Actively” means through deliberate policy, such as taxes, benefits or wage rules.
- **#19** (Quick, agree → Markets, mirror of #2) Incomes should be left to the market, even if some people end up far richer than others.
  - ?: “The market” means wages and profits set by buying and selling, rather than by the state.
- **#33** (Quick, agree → Equality) People with high incomes should pay a much higher tax rate than people on average incomes.
  - ?: The tax rate is the share of income that is paid in tax.
- **#44** (Standard, agree → Markets) Prices such as rents should be set by the market, even when they rise sharply.
  - ?: The alternative to market prices is a legal limit, such as rent control.
- **#51** (Standard, agree → Markets) Pay should be agreed between each employer and worker, without minimum levels set by law or by unions.
  - ?: Minimum pay can be set by law (a minimum wage) or by agreements between unions and employers for a whole sector.
- **#69** (Standard, agree → Equality) Large inheritances should be heavily taxed to reduce differences in wealth.
  - ?: An inheritance is money or property passed on when someone dies.

### 2. Ownership: Collective ownership ↔ Private ownership

*Measures:* Who should own firms, land, natural resources and infrastructure.

- **#5** (Quick, agree → Private ownership) Most large companies should be privately owned.
  - ?: Privately owned means owned by individuals, families or shareholders, rather than by the state, the public or the workers as a group.
- **#22** (Quick, agree → Collective ownership, mirror of #5) Most large companies should be owned collectively rather than privately.
  - ?: Collective ownership means ownership by the state, a municipality or another public body, or by the workers as a cooperative.
- **#29** (Quick, agree → Private ownership, empirical) Private companies generally run businesses more efficiently than the state does.
  - ?: Efficiency means delivering goods or services at lower cost or higher quality.
- **#40** (Standard, agree → Collective ownership) Companies that supply water and power should be owned by the public.
  - ?: “Owned by the public” can mean the state, a municipality or another public body acting for citizens.
- **#59** (Standard, agree → Private ownership) Land is best owned by private individuals and companies.
  - ?: This concerns land itself, such as farmland, building plots and forests, rather than the buildings on it.
- **#62** (Standard, agree → Collective ownership) Most large banks should be owned by the public or by their customers, rather than by private investors.
  - ?: Owned by the public means by the state, a region or a municipality. Owned by customers means a cooperative bank, such as a credit union.

### 3. Trade: Protectionism ↔ Free trade

*Measures:* How open the country should be to foreign goods, services and capital.

- **#10** (Quick, agree → Protectionism) My country should protect its own industries from foreign competition.
  - ?: Protection usually means tariffs (taxes on imports), limits on imports or subsidies for domestic producers.
- **#14** (Quick, agree → Protectionism) Foreign investors should face limits on buying companies in my country.
  - ?: This is about foreign ownership of domestic businesses, not about trade in goods.
- **#26** (Quick, agree → Free trade, mirror of #10) Trade between countries should face few tariffs or other barriers.
  - ?: Tariffs are taxes on imported goods. Other barriers include import quotas and rules that favour domestic products.
- **#48** (Standard, agree → Free trade, empirical) Countries are better off specialising in what they produce best and trading for the rest.
  - ?: Specialising means focusing on some industries and importing goods that other countries make more cheaply.
- **#55** (Standard, agree → Free trade) Companies should be free to move production to wherever costs are lowest.
  - ?: Moving production to another country is often called offshoring.
- **#66** (Standard, agree → Protectionism) My country should grow most of its own food, even if imports would be cheaper.
  - ?: This is about self-sufficiency in food.

### 4. State power: Liberty ↔ Authority

*Measures:* How much power the state should have over individual life.

- **#9** (Quick, agree → Liberty) Adults should be allowed to make risky choices that harm only themselves.
  - ?: For example dangerous sports, smoking or heavy drinking.
- **#15** (Quick, agree → Authority) Police should have broad powers to stop and search people in order to prevent crime.
  - ?: “Stop and search” means checking a person or their belongings without arresting them.
- **#30** (Quick, agree → Authority, mirror of #9) The law should protect people from their own harmful choices.
  - ?: For example through bans, compulsory safety rules or penalties for risky personal behaviour.
- **#37** (Standard, agree → Liberty) People should be free to express views that most others find offensive.
  - ?: This concerns what the law permits, not whether such views deserve agreement or respect.
- **#52** (Standard, agree → Authority) Authorities should be able to monitor online communication to prevent serious crime.
  - ?: Monitoring can include reading messages, tracking contacts or collecting browsing data.
- **#67** (Standard, agree → Liberty) Peaceful protests should be allowed even when they disrupt daily life.
  - ?: Disruption means things like blocked roads or delayed public transport, without violence.

### 5. Centralization: Centralized ↔ Decentralized

*Measures:* Where power should sit: at the national level or with regions and localities.

- **#3** (Quick, agree → Decentralized) Decisions should be made at the most local level possible.
  - ?: Local levels are municipalities, districts or regions, as opposed to the national government.
- **#23** (Quick, agree → Centralized, mirror of #3) Most rules, such as what schools teach, should be set nationally rather than by regions.
  - ?: Regions are units below the national level, such as states, provinces or cantons.
- **#35** (Quick, agree → Centralized, empirical) The national government usually solves problems better than regional or local governments.
  - ?: The national government is the central government of the whole country.
- **#41** (Standard, agree → Decentralized) Regions should be able to set their own tax rates.
  - ?: Regions are units below the national level, such as states, provinces or cantons.
- **#60** (Standard, agree → Decentralized) Regions should be able to pass their own laws on matters such as policing, health and schools.
  - ?: Regions are units below the national level, such as states, provinces or cantons.
- **#70** (Standard, agree → Centralized) Equal public services across the whole country matter more than letting regions and towns run them their own way.
  - ?: Public services here means things like hospitals, policing and social care. Regions are units below the national level, such as states, provinces or cantons.

### 6. Participation: Citizens decide directly ↔ Representatives/experts decide

*Measures:* How far collective decisions should pass from citizens to elected representatives and experts, or at the far end to rulers who need no election.

- **#1** (Quick, agree → Citizens decide directly) Citizens should be able to vote directly on important laws.
  - ?: A direct vote on a law is often called a referendum or popular vote.
- **#20** (Quick, agree → Representatives/experts decide, mirror of #1) Elected representatives, not a popular vote, should have the final say on important laws.
  - ?: Elected representatives are members of a parliament or council chosen by voters.
- **#27** (Quick, agree → Representatives/experts decide) Decisions such as setting interest rates should be left to independent experts.
  - ?: Interest rates decide how expensive it is to borrow money.
- **#45** (Standard, agree → Representatives/experts decide, empirical) Leaders who do not have to win elections often make better long-term decisions.
  - ?: Such leaders could include monarchs, appointed officials or members of a governing party.
- **#56** (Standard, agree → Citizens decide directly) Assemblies of randomly selected citizens should help decide important issues.
  - ?: Choosing citizens by lot, as for a jury, is sometimes called sortition or a citizens’ assembly.
- **#63** (Standard, agree → Citizens decide directly) Elected officials should follow the wishes of their voters, even when they personally disagree.
  - ?: This is about whether representatives act on their voters’ instructions or on their own judgement.

### 7. Social change: Progressive ↔ Traditional

*Measures:* How society should treat inherited norms and customs, for example on family, gender and the end of life.

- **#11** (Quick, agree → Traditional) Long-standing customs are better kept as they are than changed to fit modern values.
  - ?: Customs are the habits, rituals and norms a society has kept over generations.
- **#17** (Quick, agree → Progressive, empirical) Many different kinds of families can raise children equally well.
  - ?: Kinds of families include same-sex couples, unmarried couples, stepfamilies and extended families.
- **#31** (Quick, agree → Progressive, mirror of #11) Traditions should be adapted to modern values rather than kept as they are.
  - ?: Traditions are customs, rituals and norms handed down over generations.
- **#38** (Standard, agree → Traditional) It is wrong for a doctor to help a terminally ill patient end their life, even if the patient asks for it.
  - ?: Terminally ill means the illness cannot be cured and is expected to cause death. This is often called assisted dying; the question is whether it is morally right, not only what the law allows.
- **#53** (Standard, agree → Traditional) Mothers rather than fathers should be the main carers of young children.
  - ?: The main carer is the parent who spends most time looking after the children.
- **#71** (Standard, agree → Progressive) Same-sex couples should have the same right to marry as other couples.
  - ?: This is about civil marriage under the law, not religious ceremonies.

### 8. Religion and state: Separation of religion and state ↔ Religion in public life

*Measures:* What role religion should play in law and public life.

- **#8** (Quick, agree → Separation of religion and state) Religious beliefs should be kept out of law-making.
  - ?: This concerns how laws are justified and written, not whether lawmakers may be religious.
- **#13** (Quick, agree → Religion in public life) State-run schools should include lessons that teach a religious faith.
  - ?: Teaching a faith means teaching its beliefs and practices as true, as opposed to neutral teaching about religions.
- **#34** (Quick, agree → Religion in public life, mirror of #8) Laws should be based on religious teachings about right and wrong.
  - ?: Religious teachings are the guidance a faith gives on how to live, for example on family life or charity.
- **#42** (Standard, agree → Separation of religion and state) Religious organisations should fund themselves, without money from the state.
  - ?: State money includes direct payments and church taxes collected by the state.
- **#57** (Standard, agree → Religion in public life) Religious leaders should have a say in national political decisions.
  - ?: Having a say could mean formal seats in institutions or regular consultation by the government.
- **#68** (Standard, agree → Separation of religion and state) Courts and state schools should keep their walls free of religious symbols, such as crosses.
  - ?: This is about symbols the institution puts up, not about what individuals wear.

### 9. Ecology: Growth ↔ Ecological sustainability

*Measures:* What comes first when economic growth and ecological limits conflict.

- **#6** (Quick, agree → Growth) When economic growth and environmental protection conflict, growth should come first.
  - ?: Economic growth is an increase in the total goods and services an economy produces.
- **#21** (Quick, agree → Ecological sustainability, mirror of #6) Slower economic growth is a price worth paying to protect the environment.
  - ?: Protecting the environment includes preserving habitats, species, clean air and a stable climate.
- **#25** (Quick, agree → Growth, empirical) Technological progress, rather than consuming less, is the best answer to environmental problems.
  - ?: Examples of such technology include cleaner energy, carbon capture and more efficient machines.
- **#46** (Standard, agree → Ecological sustainability) Rich countries should aim to consume less, even if their economies stop growing.
  - ?: Consuming less means buying fewer goods and using less energy and transport overall.
- **#49** (Standard, agree → Ecological sustainability) Large areas of land and sea should be closed to farming, mining and building.
  - ?: Such areas are usually called protected areas or nature reserves.
- **#64** (Standard, agree → Growth) Projects that create jobs should go ahead even if they harm local nature.
  - ?: Local nature means the plants, animals and landscapes in the area of the project.

### 10. Nation and world: Nationalism ↔ Internationalism

*Measures:* Whether the nation or shared international institutions should come first.

- **#4** (Quick, agree → Nationalism) My country should make its own laws rather than share that power with international bodies.
  - ?: International bodies include the United Nations, regional unions and international courts.
- **#24** (Quick, agree → Internationalism, mirror of #4) International bodies should be able to make some rules that my country must follow.
  - ?: Examples include trade rules, human-rights courts and agreements on climate or fishing.
- **#32** (Quick, agree → Internationalism) Being a citizen of the world is an important part of how I see myself.
  - ?: This is about where your sense of belonging and loyalty lies.
- **#39** (Standard, agree → Nationalism) Schools should teach children to be proud of their country.
  - ?: This is about whether national pride should be an aim of education, not about whether history is taught.
- **#54** (Standard, agree → Nationalism) My country should put its own interests first, even at a cost to people in other countries.
  - ?: Interests include the economy, security and the well-being of its citizens.
- **#65** (Standard, agree → Internationalism) My government should care as much about people in other countries as about its own citizens.
  - ?: This is about whose well-being a government should weigh when it makes decisions.

### 11. Foreign intervention: Interventionist ↔ Restraint

*Measures:* Willingness to use military or economic pressure abroad.

- **#7** (Quick, agree → Interventionist) My country should be willing to join military action to stop mass atrocities in another country.
  - ?: Mass atrocities include genocide, ethnic cleansing and the large-scale killing of civilians.
- **#16** (Quick, agree → Interventionist) My country should put strong pressure on foreign governments that abuse human rights.
  - ?: Pressure can mean sanctions, cutting aid or breaking off diplomatic ties.
- **#36** (Quick, agree → Restraint, mirror of #7) My country should use military force abroad only if it is attacked first.
  - ?: Being attacked means a direct armed attack on your country’s territory or forces.
- **#43** (Standard, agree → Restraint) Replacing an undemocratic government should be left to that country’s own people, not to my country.
  - ?: Helping to replace a government can mean military action or arming rebels.
- **#58** (Standard, agree → Restraint) My country should be neutral rather than belong to a military alliance.
  - ?: A military alliance is a treaty in which countries promise to defend each other.
- **#72** (Standard, agree → Interventionist) My country should be willing to use military force abroad to protect its economic interests, such as trade routes.
  - ?: Here, this means interests such as shipping routes and energy supplies.

### 12. Migration: Open ↔ Restrictive

*Measures:* How freely people should be able to move to and settle in a country.

- **#12** (Quick, agree → Restrictive) My country should accept immigrants only if they clearly benefit the country.
  - ?: This is about people moving to work or settle long-term, not about refugees.
- **#18** (Quick, agree → Restrictive) People with no legal right to stay, such as rejected asylum seekers, should be deported even after living and working here for five years.
  - ?: Deportation means the state sends a person to their home country or to another country that accepts them. A rejected asylum seeker is someone whose claim to protection has been turned down.
- **#28** (Quick, agree → Open, mirror of #12) People should be free to move to my country to live and work unless there is a strong reason to refuse them.
  - ?: This is about people moving to work or settle long-term, not about refugees.
- **#47** (Standard, agree → Open, empirical) Immigration is generally good for the economy of the receiving country.
  - ?: Economic effects include the work immigrants do, the taxes they pay and the public services they use.
- **#50** (Standard, agree → Open) My country should take in refugees fleeing war, even when many arrive at once.
  - ?: A refugee is someone who has fled their country because of war or persecution.
- **#61** (Standard, agree → Restrictive) Immigrants should have to live in the country for at least ten years before they can become citizens.
  - ?: Citizenship gives full rights, such as voting and a passport.
