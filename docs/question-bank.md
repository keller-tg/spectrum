## Grouped by axis, with explainers

### 1. Distribution: Equality ↔ Markets

*Measures:* How far the state should shape who earns and owns what.

- **#2** (Quick, agree → Equality) Governments should actively reduce the gap between high and low incomes.
  - ?: “Actively” means through deliberate policy, such as taxes, benefits or wage rules.
- **#19** (Quick, agree → Markets, mirror of #2) Incomes should be left to the market, even if some people end up far richer than others.
  - ?: “The market” means wages and profits set by buying and selling, rather than by the state.
- **#33** (Quick, agree → Equality) People with high incomes should pay a much higher tax rate than people on average incomes.
  - ?: The tax rate is the share of income that is paid in tax.
- **#44** (Standard, agree → Markets) Prices such as rents should be set by the market, even when they rise sharply.
  - ?: The alternative to market prices is a legal limit, such as rent control.
- **#51** (Standard, agree → Markets) Pay should be agreed between each employer and worker, without minimum levels set by law or by unions.
  - ?: Minimum pay can be set by law (a minimum wage) or by agreements between unions and employers for a whole sector.
- **#69** (Standard, agree → Equality) Large inheritances should be heavily taxed to reduce differences in wealth.
  - ?: An inheritance is money or property passed on when someone dies.

### 2. Ownership: Collective ownership ↔ Private ownership

*Measures:* Who should own firms, land, natural resources and infrastructure.

- **#5** (Quick, agree → Private ownership) Services such as water and electricity are best provided by private companies.
  - ?: “Provided” means the companies own and run the service; the state may still set rules for it.
- **#22** (Quick, agree → Collective ownership, mirror of #5) Companies that supply water and power should be owned by the public.
  - ?: “Owned by the public” can mean the state, a municipality or another public body acting for citizens.
- **#29** (Quick, agree → Private ownership) Private companies generally run businesses more efficiently than the state does.
  - ?: Efficiency means delivering goods or services at lower cost or higher quality.
- **#40** (Standard, agree → Collective ownership) More companies should be owned by the people who work in them.
  - ?: Worker ownership is often organised as a cooperative, in which each member has a vote.
- **#59** (Standard, agree → Private ownership) Land is best owned by private individuals and companies.
  - ?: This concerns land itself, such as farmland, building plots and forests, rather than the buildings on it.
- **#62** (Standard, agree → Collective ownership) Companies that extract oil, gas and minerals should be owned by the state.
  - ?: Extraction means mining or drilling natural resources out of the ground.

### 3. Trade: Protectionism ↔ Free trade

*Measures:* How open the country should be to foreign goods, services and capital.

- **#10** (Quick, agree → Protectionism) My country should protect its own industries from foreign competition.
  - ?: Protection usually means tariffs (taxes on imports), limits on imports or subsidies for domestic producers.
- **#14** (Quick, agree → Protectionism) Foreign investors should face limits on buying companies in my country.
  - ?: This is about foreign ownership of domestic businesses, not about trade in goods.
- **#26** (Quick, agree → Free trade, mirror of #10) Trade between countries should face few tariffs or other barriers.
  - ?: Tariffs are taxes on imported goods. Other barriers include import quotas and rules that favour domestic products.
- **#48** (Standard, agree → Free trade) Countries are better off specialising in what they produce best and trading for the rest.
  - ?: Specialising means focusing on some industries and importing goods that other countries make more cheaply.
- **#55** (Standard, agree → Free trade) Companies should be free to move production to wherever costs are lowest.
  - ?: Moving production to another country is often called offshoring.
- **#66** (Standard, agree → Protectionism) My country should grow most of its own food, even if imports would be cheaper.
  - ?: This is about self-sufficiency in food.

### 4. State power: Liberty ↔ Authority

*Measures:* How much power the state should have over individual life.

- **#9** (Quick, agree → Liberty) Adults should be allowed to make risky choices that harm only themselves.
  - ?: For example dangerous sports, smoking or heavy drinking.
- **#15** (Quick, agree → Authority) Police should have broad powers to stop and search people in order to prevent crime.
  - ?: “Stop and search” means checking a person or their belongings without arresting them.
- **#30** (Quick, agree → Authority, mirror of #9) The law should protect people from their own harmful choices.
  - ?: For example through bans, compulsory safety rules or penalties for risky personal behaviour.
- **#37** (Standard, agree → Liberty) People should be free to express views that most others find offensive.
  - ?: This concerns what the law permits, not whether such views deserve agreement or respect.
- **#52** (Standard, agree → Authority) Authorities should be able to monitor online communication to prevent serious crime.
  - ?: Monitoring can include reading messages, tracking contacts or collecting browsing data.
- **#67** (Standard, agree → Liberty) Peaceful protests should be allowed even when they disrupt daily life.
  - ?: Disruption means things like blocked roads or delayed public transport, without violence.

### 6. Participation: Rule by the many ↔ Rule by the few

*Measures:* Who should make collective decisions: citizens directly or a selected few.

- **#1** (Quick, agree → Rule by the many) Citizens should be able to vote directly on important laws.
  - ?: A direct vote on a law is often called a referendum or popular vote.
- **#20** (Quick, agree → Rule by the few, mirror of #1) Elected representatives, not a popular vote, should have the final say on important laws.
  - ?: Elected representatives are members of a parliament or council chosen by voters.
- **#27** (Quick, agree → Rule by the few) Decisions such as setting interest rates should be left to independent experts.
  - ?: Interest rates decide how expensive it is to borrow money.
- **#45** (Standard, agree → Rule by the few) Leaders who do not have to win elections often make better long-term decisions.
  - ?: Such leaders could include monarchs, appointed officials or members of a governing party.
- **#56** (Standard, agree → Rule by the many) Assemblies of randomly selected citizens should help decide important issues.
  - ?: Choosing citizens by lot, as for a jury, is sometimes called sortition or a citizens’ assembly.
- **#63** (Standard, agree → Rule by the many) Elected officials should follow the wishes of their voters, even when they personally disagree.
  - ?: This is about whether representatives act on their voters’ instructions or on their own judgement.

  - ?: Traditions are customs, rituals and norms handed down over generations.
